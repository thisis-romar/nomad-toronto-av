# Lighting Fixture Identification Audit

**Trigger:** the venue supplied three fixture manuals (committed to `08-lighting/manuals/`) and,
separately, manufacturer electrical data for four fixtures. **Rev 2** folds in that electrical data
and the YF BEAM 230, which corrects the beam identification made in rev 1 — see §8.
Until now every fixture in the rig was a **generic MA profile name with no known real-world model**
(`nomad-lighting-spec-v1.md` §5, Open Item §9.3). This closes most of that gap, and opens two
questions that matter more.

**Method.** The showfile records a fixture-profile *name* and a start address per fixture. It does
**not** contain the fixture-type definitions, so a profile's internal channel order cannot be read
back from it — only its footprint, from the `…CH` in the name. Two things *can* be checked
mechanically, and they are what this audit is:

1. **Is the patched footprint a mode the real fixture can be switched to?**
2. **What is the address stride?** The gap between consecutive fixtures is independent evidence —
   it records how much room the person patching believed each fixture needed. Where the stride and
   the profile disagree, the stride is usually the truth and the profile is the mistake.

Everything numeric below is generated by `scripts/audit-dmx-patch.py`.

## §4 Finding 1 — the strobe bars: 13CH is not a mode

**The seven `LED.Strobe-BAR` fixtures are patched 13 channels apart. The Light4Me STROBE MULTI BAR
can only be set to 4, 16 or 168.** One of three things is true, and they have different symptoms:

**(a) The bars are in 16CH mode.** Each bar is then 3 channels wider than its patch slot and runs
into the bar addressed after it:

<!-- generated: python3 scripts/audit-dmx-patch.py --md -->

| Fixture | Occupies | Runs into | Shared addresses |
|---------|----------|-----------|------------------|
| LED.Strobe-BAR 6 | 383–398 | LED.Strobe-BAR 5 (starts 396) | **396–398** (3 ch) |
| LED.Strobe-BAR 5 | 396–411 | LED.Strobe-BAR 4 (starts 409) | **409–411** (3 ch) |
| LED.Strobe-BAR 4 | 409–424 | LED.Strobe-BAR 3 (starts 422) | **422–424** (3 ch) |
| LED.Strobe-BAR 3 | 422–437 | LED.Strobe-BAR 2 (starts 435) | **435–437** (3 ch) |
| LED.Strobe-BAR 2 | 435–450 | LED.Strobe-BAR 1 (starts 448) | **448–450** (3 ch) |

The shared addresses are CH14–16 of the overrun bar (RGBW-effect speed, background colour,
background dimmer) driven by CH1–3 of the next one (master dimmer, RGB strobe, red). **Symptom:
the background colour and background dimmer of five bars follow the neighbouring bar's intensity
instead of the console.** Note BAR 7 is not in the list — it sits at 357 with the 370–382 gap after
it, which is exactly the room a 16-channel bar needs. That gap was previously flagged as an
unexplained spare (`nomad-lighting-spec-v1.md` §9.5); 16CH mode would explain it.

**(b) The bars are in 4CH mode.** No collision, but only R/G/B/W respond — the console's master
dimmer, strobe and all effect attributes do nothing, and 9 of every 13 patched channels are dead.

**(c) They are not Light4Me bars.** Then some other 13CH RGBW bar is in the rig and this manual
belongs to something else.

Cases (a) and (b) both mean the MA profile does not describe the fixture, and the fix is the same:
set every bar to 16CH, re-patch on a 16-channel stride, and load a matching profile.

## §5 Finding 2 — the laser bars: 26CH is not a mode either

**The nine `Laser.BAR(6)` fixtures are patched on a 26-channel stride. The LS650 offers 11, 19
and 24.** This one does **not** collide — 24 < 26, so each bar leaves 2 dead channels and the next
bar starts clear. 18 channels are wasted across the nine bars; nothing breaks.

What cannot be checked from here is whether the profile's channel *order* matches. The showfile
carries no fixture-type definitions, so the 26-channel layout inside `8 LASER BARS 26CH` is
invisible to this audit. Two possibilities:

- The profile replicates the LS650's 24CH map with 2 pad channels → everything works, and the only
  cost is the wasted addresses.
- The profile is a different 26-channel laser bar → **every attribute lands on the wrong channel**,
  and the bars would be visibly uncontrollable rather than subtly wrong. That is easy to rule out
  by eye and presumably already has been.

There is circumstantial support for the first: the MA sub-fixture layout for each bar is **one
master plus six cells of two attributes each**, and the LS650's 24CH map gives each eye exactly two
attributes — a tilt position (CH4–9) and a laser on/off (CH14–19) — with seven whole-fixture
attributes (pan, pan fine, pan speed, all-tilt, tilt speed, master switch, strobe). 7 + 6×2 = 19
patched attributes, which is what the showfile records. The remaining 5 channels of the 24CH mode
(effect select, effect speed, motor auto-run, walk speed, reset) are simply not exposed as MA
attributes.

**Where a 26CH profile could have come from.** Panda offered this chassis in single-red,
single-blue and **RGB-mixed** variants. An RGB version needs three colour channels per eye instead
of one on/off, which is +12 channels — so a sibling variant plausibly *does* have a mode in the
mid-20s. If someone loaded the RGB variant's profile against red-only bars, the footprint would
look right and the colour attributes would land on nothing. That is a hypothesis, not a finding;
the panel check in §7 settles it either way.

> `Laser.BAR(6) 1` uses the **invert** profile and its six cell addresses descend (215→210) rather
> than ascend. That is consistent with one bar hung the other way round, and is unaffected by this
> finding.

## §6 Finding 3 — the beams: patched 14CH, but spaced 16

**This is the informative one.** The four `M.BEAM` fixtures sit at 280, 296, 312 and 328 — a
**uniform 16-channel stride** — while the profile loaded against them, `9 Sharpy Standard Lamp on`,
is 14 channels. The YF BEAM 230 offers 16CH and 20CH.

At 16CH each beam occupies 280–295, 296–311, 312–327, 328–343: **it fits the stride exactly, with
nothing left over and no collision.** The next fixture up the universe is a strobe bar at 357.

That pattern is different in kind from the strobe bars in §4. There, the stride matches the profile
(13) and the fixture needs more, so the fixtures collide. Here, **the stride matches the fixture**
(16) and the profile is 2 channels short. Someone laid this patch out knowing the beams were 16
channels wide and then attached a 14-channel profile to them. The consequence is mild but specific:
**the last two channels of every beam are unreachable from the console** — on a 16CH 230 W beam
those are typically the frost/prism-rotation and lamp/reset control block, i.e. the fixture will
light, pan, tilt, colour and gobo normally while a couple of functions simply never respond.

Nothing collides, so there is no urgency, but it is a two-minute fix: load a 16-channel profile for
that fixture and repatch on the same addresses.

## §7 What to check on site — 10 minutes, no tools

Every one of these fixtures shows its mode and address on its own LCD. Read them off; **do not
change anything until all of them are recorded** — the patch is internally consistent as it stands,
and a re-patch made on a desktop inference would leave the rig worse than it is now.

| # | Fixture | Read from the panel | What it settles |
|---|---------|---------------------|-----------------|
| 1 | Any one **strobe bar** | DMX mode (`CH04` / `CH16` / `CH168`) | The highest-value single reading in the rig. 13CH is impossible, so whatever it says is news. `CH16` ⇒ five bars are colliding (§4). |
| 2 | All seven **strobe bars** | Address on each | Expect 357, 383, 396, 409, 422, 435, 448 |
| 3 | Any one **laser bar** | Channel Mode (`11CH` / `19CH` / `24CH`) | Expect **24CH**. Also confirms whether these are the red variant or an RGB sibling (§5) |
| 4 | All nine **laser bars** | Address on each | 27, 53, 79, 105, 131, 157, 183, 209 on U1; 513 on U2 |
| 5 | Any one **moving beam** | **Model plate** and channel mode | Settles §6 outright: a 189/230 W discharge YF BEAM 230 at 16CH, or a 100 W LED LM70S at 14CH. Worth ~1.2 kW on the load schedule |
| 6 | One **moving wash** | Channel mode; confirm it is *not* the same fixture as the beams | Expect **9CH**, BETOPPER LM70S |
| 7 | **DJ-deck LED bar** | Make/model plate, mode, address | Address 11, 11CH. No manual on file, no power figure |
| 8 | **CO₂ jets and hazer** | Make/model, address, trigger method, hazer wattage | Jets are **unpatched** (address 0) — spec §9.1. Hazer wattage completes the load schedule |
| 9 | **Laser bars** | The class label on the fixture body | §9 — confirms the Class 4 declaration |

> **This is a subtotal, not a total.** 1x DJ-deck LED bar; 3x CO2 jets (x2, unpatched) + atmospheric hazer carry no power figure. Currents are real power ÷ voltage and assume unity power factor, so they are a floor: add the fixtures' actual PF (and the discharge ballasts' inrush) before sizing anything.

**Why the beam identification moves the number.** Four YF BEAM 230s are 1400–1600 W; four LM70S
would have been 400 W. Getting that one line wrong is a ~1.2 kW error — the difference between a
rig that fits a 40 A feed and one that does not. This is the practical reason §6 matters.

### Laser class — read before the next show

Each LS650 carries **6 × 500 mW at 638 nm**; 3 W of optical output per bar, **nine bars**. At
500 mW per aperture these are **Class 4** by any measure — the class where the beam is an eye and
skin hazard, including from diffuse reflections, and where audience scanning stops being a design
choice and becomes a regulated one. This repo does not currently record:

| Item | Status |
|------|--------|
| Laser class declared by the manufacturer | ❓ Confirm from the product page or the label on the fixture |
| Whether any bar can reach audience-accessible space | ❓ Unknown — fixture positions are all `0,0,0` in the showfile |
| Beam-stop / mounting-angle measures | ❓ Not documented |
| Operator responsible for the lasers during a show | ❓ Not documented |
| Applicable federal/provincial requirements and any variance held | ❓ Not documented |

Treat this as an open compliance item for whoever runs the venue, not as a lighting-design note.
It should be resolved with the venue's own safety advisor rather than from a desk.
