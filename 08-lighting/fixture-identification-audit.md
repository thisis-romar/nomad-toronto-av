# Lighting Fixture Identification Audit

**Trigger:** the venue supplied fixture manuals in three batches (all committed to
`08-lighting/manuals/`), plus manufacturer electrical data. **Rev 3** adds four manuals — YF BEAM
230, Microh LEDBAR RGB, Chauvet Hurricane Haze 2D and the Elation DP-415 pack — and the venue's
note that *some fixtures are pan/tilt-inverted duplicates*. Between them they identify **every
fixture in the rig**, confirm the beam identification rev 2 inferred, and turn up two more mode
mismatches and a device that is not a fixture at all.
Until now every fixture in the rig was a **generic MA profile name with no known real-world model**
(`nomad-lighting-spec-v1.md` §5, Open Item §9.3). This closes most of that gap, and opens two
questions that matter more.

**Method.** The showfile records a fixture-profile *name* and a start address per fixture. It does
**not** contain the fixture-type definitions, so a profile's internal channel order cannot be read
back from it — only its footprint, from the `…CH` in the name. Two things *can* be checked
mechanically, and they are what this audit is:

1. **Is the patched footprint a mode the real fixture can be switched to?**
2. **What is the address stride?** The gap between consecutive fixtures is independent evidence —
   it records how much room the person patching believed each fixture needed. Where the stride and
   the profile disagree, the stride is usually the truth and the profile is the mistake.

Everything numeric below is generated by `scripts/audit-dmx-patch.py`.

## §4 Finding 1 — the strobe bars: 13CH is not a mode

**The seven `LED.Strobe-BAR` fixtures are patched 13 channels apart. The Light4Me STROBE MULTI BAR
can only be set to 4, 16 or 168.** One of three things is true, and they have different symptoms:

**(a) The bars are in 16CH mode.** Each bar is then 3 channels wider than its patch slot and runs
into the bar addressed after it:

<!-- generated: python3 scripts/audit-dmx-patch.py --md -->

| Fixture | Occupies | Runs into | Shared addresses |
|---------|----------|-----------|------------------|
| LED.Strobe-BAR 6 | 383–398 | LED.Strobe-BAR 5 (starts 396) | **396–398** (3 ch) |
| LED.Strobe-BAR 5 | 396–411 | LED.Strobe-BAR 4 (starts 409) | **409–411** (3 ch) |
| LED.Strobe-BAR 4 | 409–424 | LED.Strobe-BAR 3 (starts 422) | **422–424** (3 ch) |
| LED.Strobe-BAR 3 | 422–437 | LED.Strobe-BAR 2 (starts 435) | **435–437** (3 ch) |
| LED.Strobe-BAR 2 | 435–450 | LED.Strobe-BAR 1 (starts 448) | **448–450** (3 ch) |

The shared addresses are CH14–16 of the overrun bar (RGBW-effect speed, background colour,
background dimmer) driven by CH1–3 of the next one (master dimmer, RGB strobe, red). **Symptom:
the background colour and background dimmer of five bars follow the neighbouring bar's intensity
instead of the console.** Note BAR 7 is not in the list — it sits at 357 with the 370–382 gap after
it, which is exactly the room a 16-channel bar needs. That gap was previously flagged as an
unexplained spare (`nomad-lighting-spec-v1.md` §9.5); 16CH mode would explain it.

**(b) The bars are in 4CH mode.** No collision, but only R/G/B/W respond — the console's master
dimmer, strobe and all effect attributes do nothing, and 9 of every 13 patched channels are dead.

**(c) They are not Light4Me bars.** Then some other 13CH RGBW bar is in the rig and this manual
belongs to something else.

Cases (a) and (b) both mean the MA profile does not describe the fixture, and the fix is the same:
set every bar to 16CH, re-patch on a 16-channel stride, and load a matching profile.

## §5 Finding 2 — the laser bars: 26CH is not a mode either

**The nine `Laser.BAR(6)` fixtures are patched on a 26-channel stride. The LS650 offers 11, 19
and 24.** This one does **not** collide — 24 < 26, so each bar leaves 2 dead channels and the next
bar starts clear. 18 channels are wasted across the nine bars; nothing breaks.

What cannot be checked from here is whether the profile's channel *order* matches. The showfile
carries no fixture-type definitions, so the 26-channel layout inside `8 LASER BARS 26CH` is
invisible to this audit. Two possibilities:

- The profile replicates the LS650's 24CH map with 2 pad channels → everything works, and the only
  cost is the wasted addresses.
- The profile is a different 26-channel laser bar → **every attribute lands on the wrong channel**,
  and the bars would be visibly uncontrollable rather than subtly wrong. That is easy to rule out
  by eye and presumably already has been.

There is circumstantial support for the first: the MA sub-fixture layout for each bar is **one
master plus six cells of two attributes each**, and the LS650's 24CH map gives each eye exactly two
attributes — a tilt position (CH4–9) and a laser on/off (CH14–19) — with seven whole-fixture
attributes (pan, pan fine, pan speed, all-tilt, tilt speed, master switch, strobe). 7 + 6×2 = 19
patched attributes, which is what the showfile records. The remaining 5 channels of the 24CH mode
(effect select, effect speed, motor auto-run, walk speed, reset) are simply not exposed as MA
attributes.

**Where a 26CH profile could have come from.** Panda offered this chassis in single-red,
single-blue and **RGB-mixed** variants. An RGB version needs three colour channels per eye instead
of one on/off, which is +12 channels — so a sibling variant plausibly *does* have a mode in the
mid-20s. If someone loaded the RGB variant's profile against red-only bars, the footprint would
look right and the colour attributes would land on nothing. That is a hypothesis, not a finding;
the panel check in §7 settles it either way.

> `Laser.BAR(6) 1` uses the **invert** profile and its six cell addresses descend (215→210) rather
> than ascend. That is consistent with one bar hung the other way round, and is unaffected by this
> finding.

## §6 Finding 3 — the beams: patched 14CH, spaced 16, losing lamp control

The four `M.BEAM` fixtures sit at 280, 296, 312 and 328 — a **uniform 16-channel stride** — while
the profile loaded against them, `9 Sharpy Standard Lamp on`, is 14 channels. The YF BEAM 230
manual (now on file) confirms 16CH and 20CH modes and gives the map:

| Ch | 16CH mode | | Ch | 16CH mode |
|---:|-----------|---|---:|-----------|
| 1 | Color wheel | | 9 | Focus |
| 2 | Blackout / Strobe | | 10 | Pan |
| 3 | Dimmer | | 11 | Pan fine |
| 4 | Gobo wheel | | 12 | Tilt |
| 5 | Prism | | 13 | Tilt fine |
| 6 | Prism rotation | | 14 | Pan and Tilt speed |
| 7 | Macro | | **15** | **Reset** ← lost |
| 8 | Frost | | **16** | **Lamp control** ← lost |

At 16CH each beam occupies 280–295, 296–311, 312–327, 328–343: **it fits the stride exactly**, with
nothing left over and no collision (the next fixture up the universe is a strobe bar at 357). The
person laying this patch out knew the beams were 16 channels wide and then attached a 14-channel
profile to them.

**What that costs is now exact rather than guessed.** The 14-channel profile stops at *Pan and Tilt
speed*. Everything that makes the fixture look right — colour, gobo, prism, frost, focus, pan, tilt,
both fine channels — is inside 1–14 and works. What is unreachable is **ch15 Reset** and **ch16
Lamp control**: on four discharge fixtures that means the lamps cannot be struck or doused from the
console, and a beam that loses its home position cannot be reset remotely. That is an operational
limitation on a 230 W discharge rig, not a cosmetic one.

Nothing collides, so there is no urgency and no re-addressing needed: load a 16-channel profile for
this fixture on the same addresses.

> This also settles rev 2's inference. The beams are **not** BETOPPER LM70S LED mini heads — that
> reading fit the 14CH profile but not the 16-channel stride. Rev 1 had it wrong; the manual now
> confirms the correction.

## §7 Finding 4 — the DJ bar: 11CH patched, 13CH fixed

`3 LED Bar 2 11CH` is patched at address 11 with four sub-fixtures: a 2-channel master at 11, then
three cells at 13, 16 and 19 on a 3-channel stride. The **Microh LEDBAR RGB** is a fixed **13**
channel fixture:

| Ch | Function |
|---:|----------|
| 1 | Function select (dimmer / strobe / fade / auto / 6 chases) |
| 2–4 | All-red, all-green, all-blue dimmer |
| 5–13 | Segments 1–3, each R / G / B |

Those **three RGB segments are exactly the profile's three cells on a 3-channel stride** — which is
what makes this the right fixture. But the profile's master is 2 channels where the fixture has 4
(function + the three all-colour dimmers), so the whole segment block is **offset by two**: the
console's cell-1 red would drive the fixture's all-blue, and so on down the bar.

Nothing collides — the bar would occupy 11–23 and the next fixture is a laser bar at 27, so the
22–26 gap absorbs it. The fix is the same shape as the beams: load a 13-channel profile at the same
start address.

## §8 Finding 5 — `2 Dimmer 00` is a switch pack, not a fixture

The three `2 Dimmer 00` entries — two CO₂ jets and the hazer — have been carried through this repo
as one-channel fixtures. The **Elation DP-415** manual reframes them: it is a **4-channel portable
dimmer/switch pack**, 120 V, **15 A total and 5 A per channel**, dual Edison sockets per channel,
9-way dip-switch addressing. The CO₂ jets and the hazer are **things plugged into that pack**, and
each `2 Dimmer 00` is a pack *channel*, not a fixture with its own DMX.

> ✅ **Confirmed by the venue 2026-08-25:** there is **one** DP-415, and **both CO₂ jets** run on
> it alongside the hazer — three of its four channels, one spare. Rev 3 inferred this; it is no
> longer an inference.

Three consequences, in order of how much they matter:

**1. The Haze 2D must not be on a dimmer channel — and it cannot be isolated.** Its manual states
"Use on Dimmer: ✗" outright. The DP-415's mode is selected by **dip switch 10 and applies to the
whole pack** — Dimmer or Switch, not per channel. With the hazer and both jets confirmed on the
*same* pack, there is no arrangement that dims the jets while switching the hazer: **the pack has
to be in Switch mode**, or the hazer is being fed a chopped waveform it is not designed for.
Read dip switch 10. This is the one finding in the audit with a single correct answer.

**2. The hazer is at 88% of its channel rating.** 533 W is 4.4 A on a channel rated 5 A. Within
spec, with very little headroom; the pack's own troubleshooting page notes per-channel fuses.

**3. "CO₂ jets unpatched (address 0)" now has a concrete fix.** They are not fixtures needing a
patch of their own — they need the DP-415's dip-switch start address, and then they are that
address + 0/1/2/3 depending on which sockets they are in. Open Item §9.1 has been asking the wrong
question since June.

**4. Three channels used, one spare.** The pack is 15 A across four 5 A channels. The hazer alone
is 4.44 A; whatever the two jets draw is bounded by the pack even though nobody has measured them
— at most 5 A each, at most 10.6 A between them. A CO₂ jet is a solenoid valve, so the real figure
should be far below that. The spare fourth channel is worth knowing about before anything else
gets added to the effects rig.

> The hazer also has its own 2-channel DMX personality (1 blower speed, 2 haze volume). It is
> patched 1CH. If what is patched is the *hazer's* DMX rather than a pack channel, then haze volume
> never responds to the console. Which of the two it is, is question 8 in §9.

### Laser class — read before the next show

Each LS650 carries **6 × 500 mW at 638 nm**; 3 W of optical output per bar, **nine bars**. At
500 mW per aperture these are **Class 4** by any measure — the class where the beam is an eye and
skin hazard, including from diffuse reflections, and where audience scanning stops being a design
choice and becomes a regulated one. This repo does not currently record:

| Item | Status |
|------|--------|
| Laser class declared by the manufacturer | ❓ Confirm from the product page or the label on the fixture |
| Whether any bar can reach audience-accessible space | ❓ Unknown — fixture positions are all `0,0,0` in the showfile |
| Beam-stop / mounting-angle measures | ❓ Not documented |
| Operator responsible for the lasers during a show | ❓ Not documented |
| Applicable federal/provincial requirements and any variance held | ❓ Not documented |

Treat this as an open compliance item for whoever runs the venue, not as a lighting-design note.
It should be resolved with the venue's own safety advisor rather than from a desk.

## §12 Inverted fixtures — what the duplicate profiles are for

The venue reports that **some fixtures are pan/tilt-inverted duplicates**. That explains a pattern
in the patch that the earlier revisions of this document guessed at and got wrong: two of the eight
MA profiles are duplicates of another profile, for fixtures hung upside down.

**Why this is corroborated rather than merely accepted.** If `6 movingwash zone` were what its name
suggests — a zoned or cell-based mode of the wash — it would have *sub-fixtures* in the export, the
way the laser bars and the DJ bar do. It has exactly one sub-fixture with nine channels, identical
to `5 NEW WASH`. The two profiles are structurally the same fixture and differ only in something
the export cannot represent, which is what an invert-only duplicate looks like from here.
`7 LASER BARS - Invert 26CH` is stronger still: it says so in the name, *and* its six cell addresses
run **descending** (215→214→…→210) where every other bar ascends — the physical mirror.

**So three fixtures are hung inverted:** `M.Wash 7`, `M.Wash 10` and `Laser.BAR(6) 1`. That is the
first orientation information this repo has held. It is not a plot — every `AbsolutePosition` in the
showfile is still `0,0,0` — but it is three fixtures' worth of real rigging fact, and it should
survive into the survey rather than being rediscovered.

**Worth raising with whoever maintains the showfile:** grandMA2 patches pan and tilt inversion
*per fixture* (Patch → Encoder/Invert), which is the usual way to handle an inverted hang. Carrying
a duplicate fixture *type* instead means the inverted units cannot be selected or edited as one
type with the rest of their group, and the profile count grows with every orientation. It works —
it is not a fault — but it is a maintenance cost, and it is the reason `6 movingwash zone` reads
like a mode when it is really a mounting.
